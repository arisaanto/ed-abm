# Literature Audit

## Status

This audit fixes the literature strategy for the Research Project 1 technical
report. It is a claim-to-source plan, not a systematic review and not manuscript
prose. It should be read together with `LITERATURE_REFERENCE_MAP.md` and
`../../writing/ARGUMENT_AND_EVIDENCE_AUDIT.md`.

Audit completed: 2026-07-26.

## 1. Verdict

The project has enough literature. The writing risk is citation sprawl, not an
evidence shortage.

The report should use a compact core bibliography to do four jobs:

1. establish that ED communication is frequent, situated, and interruption
   sensitive;
2. establish that visibility and configuration can structure opportunity,
   movement, copresence, and communication without determining their value;
3. justify a purpose-bounded ABM validation and paired stochastic intervention
   design; and
4. bound the synthetic cognitive extension as a computational design probe,
   not a substitute for human participants.

Conceptual sources may frame the relational argument. They may not be used as
empirical evidence that this model is valid or that its synthetic appraisals
describe real clinicians.

## 3. Main-Text Core Set

The following sources should carry most of the report. A source may appear in
more than one section, but the prose should normally use no more than two to
four references for one proposition.

### Introduction

- Coiera et al. (2002): communication load and interpersonal exchange in EDs.
- Naccarella et al. (2019): spatial design and ED team communication.
- Gharaveis et al. (2019): direct ED visibility and face-to-face communication
  evidence.
- Ransolin et al. (2026): current ED built-environment synthesis and local
  design caution.
- Lim and Zimring (2020): visibility-to-experience analytical distinction.
- Paul et al. (2010) or Yousefi et al. (2020): ED simulation context; use one,
  not both, unless the sentence distinguishes simulation from optimization.

### Methods

- Grimm et al. (2010): ODD-style model description.
- Sargent (2013): verification and purpose-bounded validation.
- Windrum et al. (2007): empirical ABM validation limits.
- Kleijnen (1988): paired common-random-number comparisons.
- Ten Broeke et al. (2016): sensitivity screening.
- Benedikt (1979) and Turner et al. (2001): isovists and VGA.
- Park et al. (2023): bounded memory/retrieval/reflection precedent.
- Flanagan (1954): incident-grounded appraisal structure.

### Discussion

- Gharaveis et al. (2019) and Ransolin et al. (2026): interpret visibility,
  teamwork, privacy, and workflow tradeoffs.
- Rashid et al. (2006) or Sailer and Koutsolampros (2021): connect spatial
  structure with movement and encounter.
- Gibson (1979) and Chemero (2003): frame common availability and differential
  uptake.
- Edwards et al. (1998): frame the inclusive-fit criterion.
- Hämäläinen et al. (2023), Aher et al. (2023), and Bisbee et al. (2024): bound
  synthetic appraisal claims.

### Ethics and limitations

- Bisbee et al. (2024): reduced variation, prompt sensitivity, and inferential
  mismatch in synthetic survey data.
- Santurkar et al. (2023): representational unevenness.
- Bender et al. (2021): fluency is not understanding.
- Use the independent Codex audit record as project provenance, not as a
  scholarly citation and not as human validation.

## 4. Discussion-Only Sources

These sources are useful when a specific sentence needs them. They should not
all appear merely to display breadth.

- Ulrich et al. (2008): broad evidence-based healthcare design history.
- Xuan et al. (2020), Zook et al. (2025), and Hua et al. (2012):
  visibility, privacy, communication, or corridor comparisons outside the
  exact study setting.
- Koutsolampros et al. (2019), Natapov and Hölscher (2020), Lu and Zimring
  (2012), Hadi and Zimring (2016), and Lonergan and Hedley (2016): exact
  spatial-metric definitions or limitations.
- Pan et al. (2026), Yuan et al. (2025), and Salkhi Khasraghi and Nejat (2024):
  spatial ABM precedents when distinguishing this model from adjacent uses.
- Rietveld and Kiverstein (2014): richer sociomaterial interpretation of
  affordance relevance.
- Gershman and Daw (2017), Zhu and Wholey (2018), and Argote et al. (2025):
  memory or transactive-memory interpretation.
- Marco et al. (2024), Mlinek and Pierce (1997), and Barlas et al. (2001):
  explain why privacy could not be inferred from the available traces.
- Binz et al. (2025), PersonaLLM, and Serapio-Garcia et al. (2025): explain what
  a genuine cognition or psychometric validation program would require.

## 5. Omit Unless a Sentence Specifically Needs Them

These are legitimate references, but they would make the report look broader
than its evidence.

- Anderson (1972), Batty (2001), Arcaute et al. (2022): use at most one if the
  final Introduction explicitly defines emergence.
- Merleau-Ponty (1945/2012), Lynch (1960), Whyte (1980), and Ingold (2007):
  reserve for a carefully bounded conceptual paragraph or recommendations.
- Wong (2011) and Kandinsky (1926): figure-design provenance, not scientific
  argument.
- Sensor studies by Isella et al. (2011), Vanhems et al. (2013), and Curtis et
  al. (2021): cite only if the Methods explain the empirical data-capture
  technology.
- Argyle et al. (2023), Salminen et al. (2025), and Zeng et al. (2026): useful
  context, but redundant if the stronger empirical synthetic-user cautions
  already support the sentence.

## 6. Section-Level Citation Plan

### Summary

No literature review. Cite nothing unless the university template or final
format requires citations in the summary.

### Introduction

Use four short moves:

1. ED coordination is communication-intensive and situated.
2. Built configuration structures visibility and encounter opportunities, but
   visibility is not equivalent to interaction or value.
3. Existing ED design and simulation work rarely connects observed interaction
   ecology, controlled spatial intervention, and bounded differential uptake in
   one chain.
4. State the research question and the three-part design.

Target: approximately 10-14 distinct references.

### Methods

Cite method origins at first use. The Methods should primarily describe what
was done, not repeat a related-work review.

Target: approximately 8-12 distinct references.

### Results

Results should be led by project evidence. Cite literature only when naming a
borrowed metric or protocol. Do not cite external work to make a model result
appear externally validated.

### Conclusions and recommendations

Compare the strongest findings with the closest empirical and design
literature. Separate:

- what the model established internally;
- what aligns with prior empirical work;
- what remains a design hypothesis; and
- what requires staff or post-occupancy evaluation.

Target: approximately 10-16 distinct references, with deliberate reuse from
the Introduction.

### Ethical considerations and GenAI statement

Use the synthetic-user caution literature and describe the actual project
controls. State that OpenAI Codex performed an independent model-assisted audit
of the 40 prespecified interview bundles. State that no human qualitative
review or inter-rater reliability assessment was completed.

## 7. Provenance and Review Rules

1. The official SCDH EVIDENT page establishes project scope and partnership; it
   is not a peer-reviewed study protocol.
2. Internal shadowing files establish this project's empirical input; they are
   not literature.
3. The Codex interview audit is an analysis artifact, not “human review,”
   “manual review,” or an independent human rater.
4. Synthetic first-person answers must always be labeled synthetic.
5. A conceptual citation may define a construct but cannot validate a measured
   effect.
6. A review may establish the state of evidence but cannot supply a missing
   causal estimate.
7. Recent or online-first references must be checked against the publisher
   record when the final bibliography is built.

## 10. Writing Rule

Each paragraph should have one evidential job. Cite the smallest set of sources
that performs that job, then return to the project's own evidence. The report
will be stronger if it demonstrates command of a limited, well-chosen
bibliography than if every adjacent field is represented.
