# Literature Reference Map

Online literature search performed 2026-07-03 and expanded for Part 3 on 2026-07-14 and 2026-07-17. This file is a paper-writing map, not a systematic review. Prefer the linked publisher/DOI record when checking final bibliographic formatting.

## 1. ED Communication, Teamwork, And Interruptions

**Use in this project.** Supports the premise that ED work is communication-intensive, predominantly interpersonal, interruption-prone, and spatially mediated. It motivates reason-gated F2F opportunities but does not justify treating every interruption as harmful or every extra interaction as beneficial.

| Reference | Supports | Paper use | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coiera EW, Jayasuriya RA, Hardy J, Bannan A, Thorpe MEC (2002), "Communication loads on clinical staff in the emergency department," *Medical Journal of Australia* 176:415-418. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5694/j.1326-5377.2002.tb04482.x) | Observational evidence that ED communication is frequent, mostly interpersonal, and commonly face-to-face; reports concurrent and interruptive communication. | Part 1 motivation; discussion of F2F ecology. | Different EDs, time, and event definition; not the empirical target for this model. |
| Kosits LM, Jones K (2011), "Interruptions experienced by registered nurses working in the emergency department," *Journal of Emergency Nursing* 37:3-8. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jen.2009.12.024) | Direct observation of ED nurse interruptions, predominantly face-to-face. | Interaction constraints; interruption sensitivity in Part 3. | Interruption counts do not establish error causality. |
| Kwon YE, Kim M, Choi S (2021), "Degree of interruptions experienced by emergency department nurses and interruption related factors," *International Emergency Nursing* 58:101036. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ienj.2021.101036) | Observed frequent ED nursing interruptions, with communication among patients, families, and nurses a primary source. | Grounds communication-rich opportunity ecology and its possible task burden. | Frequency at one ED does not calibrate this model's optional-contact decisions. |
| Walter SR et al. (2017), "Emergency doctors' strategies to manage competing workload demands in an interruptive environment," *Applied Ergonomics* 58:454-460. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27633242/) | Shows handling strategy varies with task, prompt, and location; communicative prompts can support treatment and workload management. | Supports context-sensitive urgency, diversion, and spatial evidence in Part 3. | Observed responses to incoming prompts are not equivalent to decisions to initiate optional contact. |
| Ratwani RM et al. (2017), "Emergency Physician Use of Cognitive Strategies to Manage Interruptions," *Annals of Emergency Medicine* 70(5):683-687. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2017.04.036) | Physicians immediately engaged 75.4% and multitasked 22.2% of observed incoming interruptions; rejection or delay was under 2%. | Reality check for interpreting action categories and protecting against claims that extreme synthetic rates describe real staff. | The study observed attending physicians receiving interruptions, whereas Part 3 models initiation at ABM-approved interruptible boundaries. Direct numerical comparison is invalid. |
| Nadon-Robert G et al. (2025), "A battle for attention: how do emergency physicians respond to interruptions? A scoping review," *Canadian Journal of Emergency Medicine*. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12628399/) | Synthesizes task switching, multitasking, deferral, acknowledgement, and rejection and identifies cognitive load, relevance, task, and environment as contextual influences. | Supports the bounded persona-by-context research question and falsification-oriented interpretation. | Evidence remains mostly observational and does not validate project personas or a monotonic urgency rule. |
| Naccarella L, Raggatt M, Redley B (2019), "The Influence of Spatial Design on Team Communication in Hospital Emergency Departments," *HERD* 12(2). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1937586718800481) | ED communication depends on visibility, connectedness, privacy, patient proximity, and spaces supporting different kinds of talk. | Part 2 rationale and discussion. | Qualitative/design evidence does not identify a universal optimal layout. |
| Joshi R et al. (2021), "Emergency Physicians' Workstation Design: An Observational Study of Interruptions and Perception of Collaboration During Shift-End Handoffs," *HERD*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/19375867211001379) | Connects workstation design, handoff interruption, and perceived collaboration. | Part 2 COCPIT framing; handoff/coordination discussion. | Focused on shift-end physician handoffs, not all ED work. |
| Gharaveis A, Hamilton DK, Pati D (2018), "The Impact of Environmental Design on Teamwork and Communication in Healthcare Facilities: A Systematic Literature Review," *HERD* 11(1). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1937586717730333) | Identifies layout, visibility, and accessibility as recurrent design factors in healthcare teamwork/communication. | Introduction; evidence-based design bridge. | Review notes a limited and heterogeneous evidence base. |
| Gurvich I, Wang L, O'Leary KJ, Van Mieghem JA (2020), "Collaboration, Interruptions and Changeover Times: Model and Empirical Study of Hospitalist Processing Times," *Manufacturing & Service Operations Management*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1287/msom.2019.0771) | Shows that collaboration can create task-switching and resumption costs, motivating explicit measurement of interruption/changeover burden. | Part 2 friction interpretation; future logging requirements. | Current ABM does not export interaction-attributable changeover time, so this mechanism cannot be claimed from existing runs. |
| Hua Y et al. (2012), "Effects of Nursing Unit Spatial Layout on Nursing Team Communication Patterns, Quality of Care, and Patient Safety," *HERD* 6(1). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/193758671200600102) | Examines communication, walking, satisfaction, safety, and organizational outcomes together across nursing-unit designs. | Part 2 tradeoff framing. | A spatial communication shift need not produce corresponding clinical or organizational improvement. |

**Do not claim:** increased F2F automatically improves safety, quality, or teamwork. Part 2 shows a redistribution of modeled interaction ecology, including potential interruption tradeoffs.

**Part 3 action-rate rule:** `engage`, `defer`, and `decline` are choices about initiating an ABM-supplied feasible contact at an interruptible decision boundary. They are not observed responses to an incoming interruption. Hospital interruption-response percentages therefore provide contextual plausibility checks, not numerical calibration targets. Extreme persona strata must be reported as designed-policy behavior with denominators and uncertainty, never as estimates of real staff prevalence or response probability.

## 8. Emergence, Affordance, Phenomenology, And Visual Explanation

**Use in this project.** These sources broaden the interpretive and visual vocabulary. They support treating the ED as an embodied system in which local rules and spatial affordances produce macroscopic patterns, and they motivate state-transition figures that reveal a system-level change without reproducing every underlying metric.

|---|---|---|---|
| Anderson PW (1972), "More Is Different," *Science* 177(4047):393-396. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.177.4047.393) | Establishes the classic argument that collective systems can exhibit higher-order properties requiring their own descriptors. | Framing interaction ecology and spatial segregation as emergent macroscopic properties. | "Emergent" should identify a measurable aggregate pattern, not serve as a synonym for surprising. |

**Part 3 personality rule:** the five project personas are designed workplace-interaction orientations. Their OCEAN display is an ordinal explanatory crosswalk derived from the explicit workplace priors; it is excluded from prompts and inferential analysis. Any future claim about synthetic Big Five personality requires a separate, preregistered psychometric validation.

**Part 3 memory implication:** a causal memory should represent a grounded encounter or information episode: who was involved, who initiated it, when and where it occurred, what operational context was present, whether contact actually occurred, what bounded information was exchanged, and what observable outcome followed. Retrieval should be explicit and auditable, prioritizing recency, current-context relevance, and prespecified importance. A rolling list of the model's own prior action labels is decision history, not an adequate episodic-memory implementation. The implemented policy retrieves at most five source-linked realized interactions from a two-hour window and requires an explicit colleague, patient-context, operational-reason, interaction-context, or recent-zone match. Same-patient retrieval across different colleagues permits a constrained transactive-memory signal without introducing invented clinical content. Free-form reflection remains post-run and non-causal.

**Repository translation rule:** retain the Generative Agents separation between stable identity, perceived events, retrieved experience, and later reflection. Retain explicit source lineage and save/replay discipline. Reject autonomous LLM planning, task decomposition, movement, unrestricted conversation, and embedding-based retrieval at every timestep because those would overwrite validated ED workflow and spend GPU budget on a different research question. The existing ABM attention limit, physical visibility gate, pair/reason cooldowns, schema validation, source hashes, and shared offline inference broker already provide the corresponding bounded mechanisms and remain authoritative.

## Paper-Level Claim Boundary

- **Part 1:** credible reproduction of selected empirical interaction patterns under one bounded ED baseline.
- **Part 2:** paired simulated counterfactuals showing how two spatial interventions alter affordance, visibility, interaction ecology, and modeled pressure.
- **Part 3:** completed trace-grounded synthetic design probes and a matched
  prompt-level architecture ablation; synthetic orientations and appraisals
  remain computational constructs rather than staff testimony.
- **Never:** synthetic staff testimony, clinical outcome evidence, universal design prescriptions, or proof that visibility is unconditionally beneficial.
